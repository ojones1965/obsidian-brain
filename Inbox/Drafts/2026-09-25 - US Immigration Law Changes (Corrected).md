# US Immigration Law Changes (Corrected)

## Summary

This is a corrected and updated version of the unreviewed local-LLM draft [[american-immigration-law-changes-20260921030600]]. That draft was written as if it were early 2024 and was cut off before the end. It also got several legal histories wrong, including the public charge rule, the 2019 transit ban, the 2024 appropriations, DACA intake and detention law. This version fixes those, then adds the major changes from January 2025 through September 2026.

This note is general information, not legal advice. Immigration policy changes quickly through executive action and court rulings, so check anything important against the primary source.

**Confidence key**
- **(web)**: checked against a source on 2026-09-25 (see Sources).
- **(gk)**: from Claude's general knowledge and not re-checked. Verify before relying on it.

## Corrections to the original draft

| Original claim | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| MPP was terminated in October 2021. | Mostly right. The October 29, 2021 memo was the one that counted. *Biden v. Texas* (June 30, 2022) upheld the termination, and MPP wound down in August 2022 (gk). DHS **reinstated MPP on January 21, 2025** (web). |
| The Circumvention of Lawful Pathways rule was blocked by "several district court preliminary injunctions," and the Supreme Court "declined to block it." | Wrong. N.D. Cal. vacated the rule on July 25, 2023, and the Ninth Circuit stayed that ruling, so the rule stayed in effect. The Supreme Court never ruled on it. N.D. Cal. **vacated it again on May 7, 2026** (*EBSC v. Trump*). (web) |
| The Supreme Court vacated the 2019 public charge rule in 2022, and Biden finalized a narrower rule in 2024. | Wrong. In March 2021, DHS stopped defending the 2019 rule and let a lower court's vacatur stand. The Biden replacement was finalized in **September 2022** and took effect December 23, 2022 (gk). That rule was **rescinded effective September 18, 2026**, with no replacement regulation (web). |
| The Supreme Court vacated the 2019 asylum "transit ban" in 2021. | Wrong. The Supreme Court actually let the rule take effect in September 2019. The D.C. federal district court vacated it on June 30, 2020 (*CAIR Coalition v. Trump*). (gk) |
| In March 2024, Congress passed a bipartisan package funding 1,000 asylum officers and 1,000 immigration judges. | Wrong. The bipartisan Senate border deal **failed** on February 7, 2024, by a vote of 49–50. It would have funded about 4,300 asylum officers and 100 judges. The actual FY2024 appropriations (P.L. 118-42 and 118-47) had no such hiring surge. (web) |
| DACA: "new applications are accepted." | Misleading. USCIS **accepts but does not process** first-time applications, and hasn't since July 2021. Renewals continue. (web) |
| Courts require bond hearings after about 6 months of detention. | Wrong as a matter of statute. *Jennings v. Rodriguez* (2018) rejected reading a 6-month bond hearing into the detention statutes. Detention law has since changed a lot (see below). (web/gk) |
| DOL finalized a rule in 2024 raising H-1B prevailing wages. | Wrong. No 2024 final rule exists; the 2021 rule was vacated (gk). DOL **proposed** higher wage levels on March 27, 2026, and final status is not verified (web). |
| No new statutes; the INA is largely unchanged since 1996. | Wrong. Examples include the REAL ID Act (2005) (gk), the **Laken Riley Act** (January 2025) and the **One Big Beautiful Bill Act** (July 2025) (web). |
| The immigration court backlog is over 3 million cases. | Right in size. TRAC counted **3,092,988** pending cases at the end of August 2026, down from about 3.3M in February 2026. (web) |

## Pre-2025 background (corrected)

- **Title 42** expulsions ended on May 11, 2023, and border processing returned to Title 8. (gk)
- **Circumvention of Lawful Pathways rule** (88 FR 31314, May 2023):
  - It presumed asylum ineligibility for people who crossed the southwest border between May 11, 2023 and May 11, 2025, unless they had a CBP One appointment or had been denied asylum in a transit country.
  - Unaccompanied children were exempt.
  - The presumption could be rebutted by a medical emergency, an imminent threat, or trafficking.
  - (web)
- **June 2024 "Securing the Border" rule:** largely vacated by the D.C. federal district court in May 2025 (*Las Americas v. DHS*). The appeal is pending. (web)
- **CHNV parole** (Cuba, Haiti, Nicaragua, Venezuela): see the 2025–2026 changes below.

## 2025–2026 developments

### Statutes
- **Laken Riley Act** (P.L. 119-1, January 29, 2025). Requires detention for noncitizens charged with theft, burglary, assaulting an officer and similar offenses. It also lets states sue over certain immigration decisions. (gk)
- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act** (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025). (web)
  - About $170.7B for enforcement through FY2029.
  - A $100 asylum filing fee, plus $100 for each year the case is pending.
  - A $550 fee for a first work permit.
  - New fees for parole and TPS.
  - A cap of 800 immigration judges.

### Border and asylum
- **Border "invasion" proclamation** (No. 10888, January 20, 2025), which blocked asylum access. The D.C. federal district court held it unlawful, and the D.C. Circuit affirmed on April 24, 2026 (*RAICES v. Noem*). The government asked the Supreme Court to review it on August 24, 2026. (web)
- **CBP One** appointment scheduling ended on January 20, 2025. The app was relaunched as "CBP Home" for self-deportation. (gk)
- **MPP** was reinstated on January 21, 2025. How much it has actually been used is not verified. (web)

### Parole, TPS and refugees
- **CHNV parole** (web)
  - Termination was announced March 25, 2025.
  - A Massachusetts federal court blocked it on April 14, 2025.
  - The Supreme Court stayed that order on May 30, 2025 (*Noem v. Doe*), so the terminations took effect.
- **TPS**
  - Terminations have covered about 13 countries. (gk)
  - *Mullin v. Doe / Trump v. Miot* (June 25, 2026, 6–3) allowed the Haiti and Syria terminations and sharply limited court review of TPS decisions. (web)
- **Refugees** (web)
  - Admissions were suspended January 20, 2025 (EO 14163).
  - The FY2026 ceiling was set at 7,500, a record low.
  - The ceiling was raised to 17,500 in May 2026.
- **Travel bans:** 19 countries under a June 4, 2025 proclamation. An expansion effective January 1, 2026 brought it to 39 countries, plus Palestinian Authority documents. (web)

### Citizenship and courts
- **Birthright citizenship** (web)
  - EO 14160 was issued January 20, 2025.
  - *Trump v. CASA* (June 27, 2025) limited universal injunctions.
  - *Trump v. Barbara* (June 30, 2026, 6–3) held that the order violates the Citizenship Clause, so it can't be enforced.
- **Alien Enemies Act**
  - Invoked against Tren de Aragua in March 2025.
  - *Trump v. J.G.G.* (April 7, 2025) and *A.A.R.P. v. Trump* (May 16, 2025) set habeas and notice requirements. (gk)
  - A Fifth Circuit panel found the invocation unlawful. The full court dismissed the case as moot on August 13, 2026, so the underlying question is still undecided. (web)

### Detention
- The BIA's *Matter of Yajure Hurtado* (September 2025) treats people who entered without inspection as ineligible for bond.
- The federal appeals courts are split:
  - The Fifth and Eighth Circuits side with the government.
  - About seven circuits, most recently the Third Circuit on August 28, 2026, disagree.
- Petitions asking the Supreme Court to decide the question are pending.
- (web)

### Enforcement
- EO 14159 (January 20, 2025) scrapped the Biden-era enforcement priorities.
- Expedited removal was expanded nationwide. That expansion was partly blocked in *Make the Road NY v. Noem*.
- Limits on enforcement at sensitive locations such as schools and churches were rescinded.
- A registration requirement took effect April 11, 2025.
- The Supreme Court allowed removals to third countries (*DHS v. D.V.D.*, June 23, 2025).
- It also stayed limits on immigration stops in Los Angeles (*Noem v. Vasquez Perdomo*, September 8, 2025).
- (gk; current status of these not verified)

### Legal immigration
- **Public charge:** the 2022 rule was rescinded effective September 18, 2026, leaving the statute and USCIS guidance (August 18, 2026) in place. (web)
- **H-1B** (web)
  - A $100,000 fee on certain new petitions (proclamation, September 19, 2025). The D.C. federal district court upheld it in December 2025.
  - A wage-weighted lottery took effect February 27, 2026, starting with the FY2027 cap season.
  - DOL's proposed wage increase (March 2026) would raise Level I from the 17th to the 34th percentile.
- **DACA** (web)
  - On January 17, 2025, the Fifth Circuit held that DACA's work permits and "lawful presence" parts are unlawful, but only as applied in Texas.
  - No order carrying out that ruling had been issued as of July 1, 2026.
  - Renewals continue.
- **F-1/J students** (web)
  - A final rule (July 17, 2026) replaced "duration of status" with fixed stays of up to 4 years.
  - A Massachusetts federal court blocked it nationwide on September 14, 2026, so duration of status continues for now.
  - The original draft's "remote study" claim is unverified. The standing rule allows one online class per term. (gk)

### States
- **Texas SB4:** the full Fifth Circuit threw out the injunction on standing grounds on April 24, 2026. A new class-action injunction followed and was stayed on May 29, 2026, so **SB4 can currently be enforced**. (web)
