# Financial Market Trends (Corrected)

## Summary

This is a corrected and updated version of the unreviewed local-LLM draft [[financial-trends-for-investment-20260921030826]]. That draft is dated "October 2023" but cites 2024 data. Its main story, "higher for longer, now pausing or cutting slowly", held through 2025 but no longer describes the market.

As of September 2026, the backdrop has changed:
- **Energy shock:** the Iran war (from late February 2026) closed the Strait of Hormuz and pushed oil sharply higher.
- **Rates rising again:** inflation has re-accelerated. The Fed and ECB have started raising rates again, and the 10-year Treasury yield is at its highest since 2007.

This note is general market information. It is **not investment advice**, and it leaves out the original's portfolio-recommendation section.

**Confidence key**
- **(primary)**: checked on the primary source page (Fed, ECB, BoE, BLS, BEA, Eurostat, Treasury) on 2026-09-25.
- **(web)**: from search-result summaries or secondary sources. Verify exact figures.
- **(gk)**: from Claude's general knowledge and not re-checked.

Economic data is published with a lag. CPI and PCE figures describe a month that ended 2–4 weeks earlier.

## Corrections to the original draft

| Original claim | Correction |
| --- | --- |
| Central banks have moved from hiking to "hold or slow cut." | True for 2024–2025, but it has now **reversed**. The Fed raised rates on 2026-09-16 and the ECB raised twice in 2026. The BoE is holding, with 3 of its members voting to raise. |
| Inflation is cooling but "sticky in services and housing." | Out of date. Inflation has **re-accelerated, driven by energy**: US CPI 3.4%, euro area 3.2%, UK 3.1% (August 2026). |
| The 10-year Treasury is at "multi-year highs." | True again, and more so. About 5.16–5.17% on 2026-09-25, **highest since June 2007**. |
| Small caps are lagging, and a comeback is possible. | They lagged again in 2025. **In 2026 they are leading**: the Russell 2000 is up about 20–23% YTD, reportedly its best year since 2003. |
| High-yield defaults are expected to rise. | Not in public high-yield bonds. The US default rate is about 1.0% and spreads are tight at about 270 bp. Credit stress has moved to **private credit**. |
| Tokenization is "limited to institutional pilots." | Out of date. Tokenized real-world assets (bonds, funds and similar) total about $22–34B, and there is now a US stablecoin law (GENIUS Act, July 2025). |
| Private equity dry powder is at a record high. | Dry powder is still over $1.1T, but the bigger story in 2026 is **redemption gates and liquidity stress** in private credit. |
| Soft vs. hard landing. | There has been no US recession. The better description now is slow growth with renewed inflation, a mild form of stagflation. |

## Macro and central banks

### Federal Reserve
- **2024:** cut 100 bp to 4.25–4.50%. (web)
- **2025:** three cuts, in September, October and December, reaching 3.50–3.75%. (web)
- **2026:** held through the first half. **Raised 25 bp on 2026-09-16 to 3.75–4.00%**, the first increase since 2023. (primary)
- **Chair:** Powell's term ended 2026-05-15. **Kevin Warsh** has chaired meetings since June 2026. His exact start date is not verified. (web)

### ECB (deposit rate)
- **June 2024 – June 2025:** cut eight times, from 3.75% to 2.00%.
- **2026:** raised to 2.25% (effective 2026-06-17) and then **2.50% (effective 2026-09-16)**.
- (primary)

### Bank of England
- Cut from 5.25% (August 2024) to **3.75% (December 2025)**. (web; dates of the cuts in between not verified)
- **Held on 2026-09-16 by a 6–3 vote**, with three members voting to raise. (primary)

### Inflation
- **US CPI, August 2026:** 3.4% y/y. (primary)
  - BLS lists core CPI (excluding food and energy) at 2.4%. ⚠️ That is unusually low next to core PCE of 3.3%, because core PCE usually runs below core CPI. Check the BLS table.
- **US PCE, July 2026:** headline 3.7%, core 3.3%. (primary)
- **Euro area HICP, August 2026:** 3.2%, with energy up 14.3%. (primary)
- **UK CPI, August 2026:** 3.1%. The BoE projects a peak above 4% in early 2027. (web)

### Growth and jobs
- **US real GDP:** +2.1% annualized in Q1 2026 and +1.5% in Q2. (primary)
- **August 2026 payrolls:** +162k. Unemployment 4.1%. (primary)
- There has been no recession to date.

### Rates and fiscal
- **10-year Treasury:** about 5.16–5.17% (2026-09-25), the highest since June 2007. (web)
- **US credit rating:** Moody's cut the US to Aa1 on 2025-05-16. (web)
- **US gross debt:** passed $40T on 2026-08-18. (primary)

## Energy and geopolitics
- **Iran war (from late February 2026):** closed the Strait of Hormuz. The IEA calls it the largest supply disruption in history.
- **Brent crude:** went from about $72 to a peak near $120, and was about $106 on 2026-09-25.
- Talks on a phased reopening are ongoing.
- (web)

## Trade and tariffs
- **"Liberation Day" tariffs (2025-04-02):** the S&P 500 fell about 19% from its February peak, then recovered by about late June 2025. (web)
- **Supreme Court ruling (2026-02-20):** struck down the tariffs imposed under the emergency powers law (IEEPA) in *Learning Resources v. Trump*. The trade court ordered refunds. (web)
- **Replacement tariff:** a 10% global tariff under Section 122 from 2026-02-24. The trade court ruled it unlawful on 2026-05-07, but it is still being collected while the appeal proceeds. (web)

## Asset classes

### Stocks
- **Concentration:** the top 10 companies were about 41% of the S&P 500 at the end of 2025, a record, and about 37–40% in 2026. (web)
- **AI spending:** big-tech data-center spending guidance for 2026 totals about $725B, or close to $800B including leases. This is an analyst total, not from company filings. (web)
- **AI bubble debate:** there have been sharp pullbacks but no crash.
  - A June 2026 selloff, and a second Nasdaq correction by July.
  - A selloff after Alphabet's July report raised questions about its capital spending.
  - (web)
- **S&P 500:** reached record highs through late August 2026, above 7,600 and up about 13% YTD. The late-September level is not verified. (web)
- **Russell 2000:** hit a record of about 3,053 in August 2026, up about 20–23% YTD. (web)

### Bonds and credit
- **High yield:** the US spread over Treasuries is about 266–273 bp and the yield about 7.5% (September 2026). (web; FRED series)
- **Defaults:** the US default rate on below-investment-grade bonds was about 1.0% for the year through August 2026. (web)
- **Private credit:**
  - Redemption gates at Blue Owl (February 2026) and at Apollo's $25B business-development company (March 2026).
  - Q2 2026 redemption requests at non-traded business-development companies reached about 12.4% of assets, a record.
  - About 10% of those loan books pay interest in extra debt instead of cash.
  - (web; industry sources)

### Real estate
- **Office CMBS delinquency:** about 11.7% (August 2026, Trepp), near the record.
- **Special servicing** (loans handed to specialists because of trouble): 11.42%, the highest since 2013.
- (web; check against Trepp directly)

### Commodities, currencies and crypto
- **Gold:** record of about $5,597/oz on 2026-01-29, and about $4,293 on 2026-09-25. ⚠️ That implies a drop of about 23%; verify it. (web)
- **Dollar index (DXY):** fell about 9–10% in 2025, its worst year since 2017. About 101.2 on 2026-09-24. (web)
- **Bitcoin:** record of about $126k in October 2025, about $78k in early September 2026, and about $84–87k in late September. US spot ETFs have had net outflows of about $464M YTD. (web)

## Tokenization, crypto and ESG regulation
- **Tokenized real-world assets:** about $22–34B, not counting stablecoins (May 2026; figures differ by method). Tokenized Treasuries are about $10–15B. BlackRock's BUIDL fund is about $2.8B. (web)
- **GENIUS Act (US stablecoin law):** enacted July 2025. The detailed rules are being drafted through 2026 and must take effect by 2027-01-18 at the latest. (primary: Treasury and Federal Register)
- **CLARITY Act (US crypto market-structure bill):** stalled in the Senate. (web)
- **MiCA (EU crypto law):** its transition periods ended 2026-07-01, with no extensions. (primary: ESMA)
- **EU "SFDR 2.0" (sustainable-investment disclosure rules):**
  - The Commission proposed it 2025-11-20.
  - The Council agreed its position 2026-06-24, and Parliament's economics committee on 2026-09-10.
  - A final deal is expected around Q4 2026 to Q1 2027.
  - (web)
